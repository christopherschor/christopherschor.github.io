Shutting You Out

An original instrumental composition exploring psychological tension, evolving electronic textures, unconventional instrumentation, and the interplay between musical structure and sonic space. Drawing on influences ranging from Vangelis to Daniel Lopatin and John Medeski, the composition uses timbre, dynamics, and spatial relationships to develop tension without relying on conventional cinematic gestures.

Original Composition | Video Game Scoring & Compositional Development Study

From Piano Motif to Orchestral Exploration

An evolving compositional study tracing the development of a simple piano motif into a more elaborate harmonic and orchestral arrangement. What began as an intuitive musical idea revealed unexpected harmonic relationships, particularly an ambiguous diminished-chord structure that suggested possibilities beyond the original composition. Subsequent iterations explore those relationships through expanded voicings, shifting harmonic contexts, and the introduction of orchestral instrumentation, particularly strings. Presented in three stages, the study illustrates an exploratory approach to composition in which harmonic complexity, arrangement, and musical identity emerge through experimentation rather than a predetermined structure.

Related to Video Game Concept Soundtrack, an ongoing project to be presented in a separate entry.

Original Composition | Video Game Audio & Experimental Sound Design

Interconnected Delay Networks — Rhythmic & Textural Exploration

An exploration of how relatively simple rhythmic and melodic material can be transformed through analog coloration and an interconnected network of delays and reverbs. The initial experiment uses a programmed drum pattern processed through an inexpensive vintage Realistic mixer, whose saturation characteristics and differences between its left and right channels produce an unexpectedly wide stereo image from a mono source.

Inspired partly by dub production techniques, the effects architecture uses automated sends to feed selected portions of the source material into delays that interact with one another. Reverbs are employed primarily for their aesthetic and textural qualities rather than conventional spatial simulation. As echoes accumulate and collide, the network introduces new rhythmic events and relationships, allowing the original material to evolve beyond its programmed structure.

The second experiment applies the same system to sustained synthesizer tones. Unlike percussion, these sounds offer fewer distinct attacks for the delays to manipulate. By shaping the sends much like a synthesizer’s amplitude envelope, individual fragments of sustained notes can be introduced into the network, generating rhythmic movement and unexpected melodic interactions without relying on conventional modulation.

A further direction for this system involves short, pitch-unstable feedback delays functioning as resonators, creating new timbres from sounds already established within a composition. Beyond their sonic possibilities, these transformations could subtly reintroduce familiar material in altered forms, reinforcing the identity of a musical environment without explicitly repeating earlier motifs.

The effects network has been preserved as a reusable session, with plans to develop it into a performance-oriented template using MIDI-controlled sends.

Composition, sound design, effects architecture, routing, automation, mixing, and production: Christopher Schor
